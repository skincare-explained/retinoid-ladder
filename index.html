Retinal vs retinol · the guide from the reel

The Retinoid Ladder

Your skin uses neither retinol nor retinal directly. It turns both into retinoic acid first. Retinol takes two steps to get there, and retinal takes one. That makes retinal faster and stronger, and also more likely to irritate. So the right product depends on where your skin is right now.

Retinolretinol → retinal → retinoic acid2 conversions. Gentler and slower.
Retinalretinal → retinoic acid1 conversion. Faster, and more likely to sting.

Night

  1. Cleanse gently and pat skin fully dry.
  2. Apply a pea-sized amount of retinoid to the whole face. Keep it away from the eye corners, nostrils and lips.
  3. Follow with moisturizer.

Morning

  1. Rinse or cleanse gently.
  2. Moisturize.
  3. Apply broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher every day, even when it's cloudy.
Skin type guide

Adjust for your skin

Dry

Try the sandwich method: moisturizer, then retinoid, then moisturizer again. A ceramide cream helps. Stay at Stage 1 a little longer.

Sensitive

Start with 1 night a week. Patch test behind the ear or along the jaw for 3 nights first. Low-dose encapsulated retinol is the kindest start.

Oily or acne-prone

You can often move up sooner. Use a light gel moisturizer. Skip AHA or BHA exfoliants on retinoid nights.

Combination

Use the full amount on oily areas and less on dry cheeks. Add more moisturizer where your skin feels tight.

Purging can happen in the first 4 to 6 weeks: small breakouts where you usually get them. If you see burning, swelling, or a rash in new places, stop and step back.

Skip retinoids or ask a doctor first if

  • You are pregnant, breastfeeding, or trying to conceive.
  • You have eczema, rosacea, or a skin condition you're being treated for.
  • You already use a prescription retinoid such as tretinoin or adapalene. Follow your doctor's plan instead.
